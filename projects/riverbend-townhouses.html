Riverbend Townhouses

Three dwellings on a flood-affected site — where the ground floor level wasn't a design choice, it was a planning control.

Summary

A three-dwelling development for an investor client on a site within a flood planning area. Feasibility, levels and drainage design were all shaped by the flood controls — and the project's profitability depended on getting them right the first time.

Client brief & constraints

  • Brief: Three two-bedroom townhouses with garages, targeting downsizer tenants.
  • Constraint — flood: Flood planning levels set minimum floor heights and limited the usable ground plane.
  • Constraint — drainage: Overland flow paths across the site had to be maintained and formalised.
  • Constraint — staging: The client needed dwellings completed sequentially for staged settlement and rental income.

Scope of work

Construction of three townhouses with elevated floor levels, formalised overland drainage through the site, retaining structures at the rear boundary, and staged completion to the client's settlement programme.

Approach & key decisions

The flood levels effectively removed a conventional ground-floor solution, so the design worked with elevated living and under-croft garaging. We sequenced the three dwellings so the first could complete and lease while the third was still in frame — matching the client's finance structure. Drainage certification was chased as a programme-critical item because everything downstream — literally — depended on it.

Outcome

Staged completions aligned with the client's settlement plan; all three dwellings tenanted on completion. Measurable outcomes will be published only if verified and approved by the client.
