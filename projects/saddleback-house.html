Saddleback House

A site that fell three metres from street to rear boundary. The design followed the fall — and the budget followed the design.

Summary

A three-bedroom custom home for clients moving from the city to a rural-fringe block. The brief was economical in the best sense: nothing wasted, nothing unnecessary, everything considered.

Client brief & constraints

  • Brief: A modest three-bedroom home with a workshop space and a covered outdoor area capturing the view.
  • Constraint — slope: A three-metre fall across the block made a flat slab costly and disruptive.
  • Constraint — budget: The clients had a firm ceiling, established through their finance, and wanted design decisions stress-tested against it.
  • Constraint — services: The site was beyond mains gas; all-electric design with solar-ready roof was required.

Scope of work

Construction of a split-level home stepping with the site: suspended floors over the fall, workshop level at grade, solar-ready roof with future-proof conduit, and a covered outdoor room oriented to the view corridor.

Approach & key decisions

At feasibility, the original single-level concept required retaining walls and significant fill. We modelled the alternative — a stepped split-level — which removed the retaining, halved the earthworks, and brought the project back under the clients' ceiling. That single decision, made before contract, was the project's most valuable.

Outcome

Delivered within the established budget envelope, with the earthworks saving preserved rather than absorbed. Measurable outcomes will be published only if verified and approved by the client.
