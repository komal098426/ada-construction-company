Why Builder Quotes Differ — and How to Compare Them

Three quotes, wildly different prices, the same drawings. Here's what's actually going on — and how to compare quotes honestly.

Article · ADA Construction Group Pty Ltd · [Publish date to be confirmed]

A quote is an answer to a question

When three builders quote the same project at very different prices, the usual conclusion is that someone is cheap, someone is expensive, and someone is honest. The real explanation is usually more mundane — and more useful to understand.

A quote is an answer to a question: "what will it cost to deliver this?" If the documentation defines "this" completely, every builder is answering the same question, and prices converge. If the documentation is ambiguous — a drawing without a specification, a spec that doesn't match a schedule, a note that says "builder to confirm" — then each builder fills the gaps with their own assumptions. Different questions, different answers, different prices. Nobody lied; everybody answered something different.

Where the ambiguity hides

  • Items drawn but not specified — a feature wall shown with no material or finish.
  • Site costs — some builders carry generous allowances; others price optimistically and adjust later.
  • Provisional sums — same number, different scope: a "$5,000 PS" for a bathroom means different things to different builders.
  • Exclusions — the cheapest quote is often the thinnest scope. It's not wrong; it's just answering less.
  • Level of documentation of the design itself — a concept-stage quote is an estimate of an estimate.

How to compare quotes properly

The fix isn't finding a "better" builder — it's putting all the quotes on one baseline. Three steps:

  1. Request itemised quotes to a fixed scope. Give every builder the same scope document and require the same breakdown structure.
  2. Interrogate the differences, not the total. The total tells you nothing. The line-item differences tell you everything — one builder's retaining allowance versus another's tells you what each is assuming about your soil.
  3. Normalise the exclusions. List every exclusion from every quote in one table, then price the union. The real comparison is quote-plus-what-it-excludes.
The cheapest quote is the one that, after you add back everything it excluded, is still the cheapest — and that accounts for what the site will actually require.

The uncomfortable option

Sometimes the honest answer is: the documentation isn't ready to be quoted against. Going back to complete the documentation costs money now and saves more later — the variations a builder couldn't have priced at quote stage get designed out instead of argued about on site. That's what our pre-construction review is for, and it's why one of our case studies is a review rather than a build.

Questions that cut through

  • "What allowances did you build in, and for what?"
  • "What are the three biggest risks to this price?"
  • "What's excluded that the other quotes included?"
  • "What would you want clarified before you'd stand behind this as a contract price?"

A builder's willingness to answer these in writing — before you've signed anything — is itself a quote of a different kind. Bring your quotes to us and we'll put them on one baseline — even if the builder you choose isn't us.
