What to Prepare Before Contacting a Builder

You don't need plans, a budget, or even a firm idea. You need five things — and none of them cost money.

Article · ADA Construction Group Pty Ltd · [Publish date to be confirmed]

People often apologise when they first contact us: "I know it's early, we don't have anything yet." But early is the best time to talk. What makes a first conversation useful isn't documentation — it's five small things you can sort in an afternoon.

1. The address — not the vision

Give a builder the property address and they can start thinking about the real constraints: slope, access, neighbourhood character, likely soil class, which planning controls probably apply. A vision statement helps; an address helps more. If you don't own the site yet, the suburb and a rough block description does most of the same work.

2. One honest sentence about scope

"We're thinking a new build, but it might end up a big reno — we're not sure which." That sentence is perfect. It tells the builder which two conversations to have. You don't need to have decided; you need to have named what's undecided.

3. The problem, not the solution

Builders and designers can iterate solutions; they can't iterate your problems. "We need a second living area because the teenagers have taken over ours" tells us more than "we want a rumpus room with bifold doors." Bring the problem; let the professionals earn their keep on the solution.

4. A number, even a rough one — even just a ceiling

"We don't know what these cost" is fine and true. But almost everyone knows their limit. Say it. A builder who knows your ceiling will tell you honestly whether your hopes fit under it — before you've spent money on design that doesn't. A builder who doesn't know it can't protect it.

5. Whatever you already have — and a note of what you don't

Old plans, a council letter, a soil test from a previous owner, photos of the damp corner — bring them all. And the list of what you don't have matters just as much: "no survey yet, no designer yet, no idea about overlays" is a to-do list a good builder will help you build.

What you should NOT prepare

  • A fixed brief. Certainty you don't have yet becomes a constraint you'll pay to remove.
  • Quotes you've collected elsewhere to "check against". They're answers to unknown questions — bring them later, with documentation, where they can be compared properly.
  • Answers. You're the client; bring questions.

What the first conversation should feel like

Mostly you talking, mostly us asking. If a builder spends your first meeting talking about themselves — their projects, their process, their quality — and not about your site and your constraints, that's data. The right first conversation ends with you knowing more than you went in with. Ours end with a written follow-up of what we heard and what we recommend next — start one here.
